I have an app idea but no money. What are my options?

Less money than you think is needed, if the first version is small. Here are the honest options, cheapest first, with what each one really costs you.

First, shrink it

Most app ideas are priced as if they need accounts, a phone app, a dashboard and a team. The first version needs none of that. It needs one page that does one thing, and one way to pay for it. A web app works on every phone without an app store, and it can be live on a domain that costs about as much as a lunch.

If you can't yet say your idea in one paragraph, start there. This free page writes it with you. For every step in order, see how to turn an idea into an app.

Option 1: build it yourself with AI tools

Costs: little money, a lot of evenings.

Today's AI coding tools can write most of a small web app if you can describe it clearly and are patient with errors. The hard part is rarely the code. It is the part after: getting strangers to find it. Plan for that from day one, or it will sit unseen.

Option 2: find a partner

Costs: half of it, and time finding them.

Plenty of ideas-people post looking for a developer who will work for a share of profits. Developers get many of these offers and accept few, because the idea alone carries little weight. If you go this way, bring something a builder lacks: customers you can reach, an audience, or expertise in the problem.

Option 3: pre-sell it

Costs: a page and some nerve.

Describe it on a simple page and ask people to pay or sign up before it exists. If nobody will, you have learned something for almost nothing. If they will, you have money to build with.
