# Actual, 29 Oct: "Put yourself in it" (vibecider4)

Plan: `plan/2026-10-29-dailies-you-in-it.md`. The boss's words that set it
(28 Oct): a scrolling site alone "is not impressive to me at all"; what is,
is the creator talking about the page, overlaid on top of it as it scrolls.

## What shipped (live on tap.bananafest-destiny.com/make/)
- Under a read page, a new card, "Put yourself in it": drop a 10–90 s video
  of yourself talking about the page (it needs sound). Dailies photographs
  the page with Cloudflare's Browser Rendering (one full-page phone
  screenshot plus where its headings, buttons and text sit, kept in KV for
  a day per URL), transcribes your sound the way clips are, asks GPT where
  the page should be scrolled while you say each thing (up to 8 stops),
  and renders in your browser: the page scrolling (hold, then glide), you
  cut out of your background by MediaPipe's selfie segmenter, bottom-centre
  with a soft shadow, your words as captions above you, the site's name in
  a pill on top. Three layouts: Cutout, Bubble (a circle, no cutout) and
  Split (page on top, you below). The same side panel renders, downloads
  and drafts to Buffer; title, caption and hashtags come from what you said.
- Worker: `POST /api/shot` (10 a day per account, cached by URL, 12 MB
  limit, a 390×4000 viewport if the full page fails) and `POST /api/scroll`
  (counts against the script cap). Two new secrets on the worker,
  `BR_TOKEN` and `CF_ACCOUNT_ID` (names only here).
- Privacy page: a new section, "Putting yourself in it".
- `clips.js` now exports its open/decode/listen/sound/put/lines pieces so
  `you.js` reuses them; clips behave as before. Captions can be placed at a
  given height (above the person).

## Numbers
- Cutout spike, headless Chrome with SwiftShader, 288×512 work canvas:
  CPU delegate 36.5 ms a frame in one pass, 66 ms with the crop second pass
  (needed for a small figure, or the head goes); the GPU delegate 229 ms
  because of readback. So a probe runs two trial frames and picks CPU when
  one takes over 80 ms.
- First full run, a 30 s public-domain NASA interview clip (in the
  scratchpad, not committed) over the Comic Cover page: the card was up in
  7.7 s (screenshot and transcription run together), GPT set 4 scroll
  stops, the render took 286 s and made 11.8 MB.
- Why 286 s: timing every drawing step at 1080×1920 gave 0.0–0.1 ms each;
  the software H.264 encoder in headless Chrome took 387 ms a frame. The
  encoder is the whole cost, as it is for clips; a real Chrome with a
  hardware encoder is far faster. No drawing change was needed. The mask
  is now found at most 22 times a second and reused between frames, since
  a talking person barely moves.
- Second run, 12 s, screenshot from the cache: card in 4.6 s, render 97 s,
  4.8 MB. Where the clip fades to black the cutout falls back to the
  bubble, as designed.
- What it looks like: the test figure is small and far away, so upscaled,
  and its legs ghost against a busy page. A phone selfie, close and lit, is
  the easy case for the model. Not yet tried with a real phone video; that
  is the boss's try.

## Live checks after deploy
`/make/` 200 with the card; `/make/you.js` 200; `/privacy` with the new
section; `POST /api/shot` with no session → 401.

# Actual, 29 Oct, later: record it here, the pointer, music (vibecider4)

Plan: `plan/2026-10-29-dailies-record.md`, the four things the first plan
left. The boss's word: "go for the next steps".

## What shipped
- **Record it here.** Under "Put yourself in it", "Or record it here" opens
  a box: a text area for what you'll say, "Write one for me" (new worker
  route `POST /api/say`: GPT writes one spoken paragraph from the page for
  20/30/45/60 s, in the first person, no hashtags; counts as a script run),
  a pace slider (100–200 words a minute), then the camera preview
  (mirrored, 9:16 on a phone camera, whatever the camera gives otherwise)
  with the words rolling over its top half at that pace after a one-second
  hold, a 3-2-1 count, a timer with a red dot, and a Stop. The take plays
  back; "Use this take" feeds it to the same path as a dropped file.
  MediaRecorder writes MP4 (Chrome, Edge, Safari); Firefox only writes WebM,
  so it gets a message to drop a file instead. The recorded MP4 is
  fragmented, which the mp4box reader used to see as zero samples; `open()`
  now reads such a file whole (under 300 MB) and the Opus codec string is
  lowercased for the decoder.
- **The screenshot ahead of time.** The page is photographed as soon as a
  video is picked, dropped or the camera starts, not when "Make the video"
  is pressed; one in-flight request per URL.
- **Music under the voice.** The same music control as the link cards
  (file, start, volume); mixed at 0.3× under the voice with the usual
  fade in and out.
- **A pointer that presses what you name.** The planner's keyframes now
  carry `tap: {x, y}` when a segment really says to press, tap or click a
  named element (the element list sent to GPT has positions and sizes; the
  tap is the element's centre). A gold disc with a white rim glides in from
  the lower right as the page glides, a ring pulses when the page lands,
  and it fades 1.6 s later. Drawn over the person, like a cursor in a
  screen recording, else the person hides the button. The card's note says
  how many buttons get pressed.
- Privacy: camera and microphone only while the box is open; the take stays
  on the device; "Write one for me" sends the page text to OpenAI.

## Numbers (local dev server, headless Chrome for Testing, fake camera fed
## a public-domain NASA clip, never committed)
- Script written in 3.1 s (78 words for 30 s). Camera to first frame under
  a second. Prompter rolled 42 px by 5 s at 150 wpm. A 10.6 s take at
  360×640 (the fake camera's size) opened, transcribed, planned: card in
  4.4 s with 4 scroll stops; render 85 s; 4.8 MB, H.264 + Opus.
- Asked straight, the planner put a tap on "Make my cover" when the words
  said "press Make my cover", and on "Download the cover" for "download it
  print size"; none when nothing was named.
- Pointer and music run (talk clip dropped, two taps forced into the plan,
  a 440 Hz test tone as the song): render 102 s; the tone measures 4–5×
  its neighbouring frequencies in the output and under 1× in a render
  without music, so the mix is in. The first pointer was white on a white
  page and under the person: invisible. Now gold, rimmed, on top.

## The look, redone after the boss saw the frames
The boss, shown eight frames of the first pointer-and-music render: "I
mean, thats not good enough. Look at those screenshots". What they saw:
the person centred over the page and hiding it, legs ghosting against page
text, captions printed over the page's own words, and a pointer that could
not be found. The frame is now composed instead of layered:
- The page scrolls inside a rounded phone-shaped window (840 wide, 1110
  tall) on a backdrop made from the page's own colours, blurred and
  darkened, with a vignette. The site's name sits in its pill above the
  window.
- The person is cut out bottom-right, at most 46% of the frame tall and
  48% wide, standing in front of the window's lower corner, never over its
  middle. The bubble layout keeps the circle bottom-left.
- Captions are a dark rounded box beside the person, left-aligned, 72 px
  uppercase, first line white and the rest gold, lifted 190 px off the
  bottom so TikTok's own overlay does not cover them. The split layout
  uses the same box over the chest, and shows a portrait clip from near
  its top so the face is in the band; its pill has a dark bar instead of
  sitting on the page heading.
- The pointer stays gold with a white rim, drawn over the person, inside
  the window.
- The card's preview is drawn at the first press (or a few seconds in),
  not at one second, so it shows the page scrolled and the pointer down.
- Test footage: the full-body NASA clip was the worst case for the
  segmenter (legs, a busy room). A head-and-shoulders crop of the same
  clip, scaled to 540×960 like a phone selfie, is now the test; still not
  a real selfie.
- Frames of the new render were put in front of the boss before this
  went live.

## Not done
- A real phone, a real face, Safari: the boss's try. Firefox can't record
  here by design.
- The recorded take's length shows "11 s" for 10.6 s; fine.

# Actual, 29 Oct, later still: the three edits (vibecider4)

The boss asked what a socials app would be wanted for; I named three
things, the boss said "Go for it." Plan: `plan/2026-10-29-dailies-edit.md`.

## What shipped
- **Captions that pop word by word.** The word being said is gold and
  grows to 1.15× as it starts, settling at 1.05× a fifth of a second
  later; the rest of the chunk is white. The popped word pushes its
  neighbours aside (the first strip had it overlapping them). Clips and
  "Put yourself in it" use the speech-to-text's word times; link videos
  share a chunk's time among its words by length. Every card has a
  "Captions" choice: Word by word (default), A few words at a time, Off.
- **Pauses cut.** While the sound is read for the words, its loudness is
  kept (50 readings a second). A pause is a stretch quieter than the
  person's level (measured on the clip itself) that no word sits in, over
  half a second; it is cut to about a fifth of a second, and the quiet
  before the first word and after the last goes too. The picture skips the
  cut stretches; the sound is laid end to end with 15 ms fades; captions,
  scroll stops and the pointer move with the cuts. In the clip layouts the
  picture alternates between two zooms (1× and 1.08×) at each cut, so it
  reads as an edit. A "Cut the pauses" checkbox on each card says how many
  and how long, e.g. "(2 pauses: 26 s instead of 28 s)".
- **Fill follows the person.** The selfie segmenter (now one module,
  `public/make/seg.js`, shared with the cutout) finds the biggest figure 4
  times a second; the crop eases after their head with a dead zone and
  jumps at a cut. A wide video defaults to Fill when a person is found in
  2 of 3 frames checked, and stays on Fit when not (a screen recording).
  "Fill: crop to the middle" is still there.
- Copy on the home card and /make/ says what the clip and "you" tools do
  now.

## Tests (local dev server, Chrome for Testing, NASA public-domain clip)
- A 75 s talk with the speaker 22% from the left: the clip crop keeps the
  face in every frame (one-a-second strip), captions pop on the word. The
  first card, 27 s, rendered to 25.6 s; headless render 143 s.
- The same talk with the frame panning so the speaker drifts across:
  the crop follows; 28 s rendered to 26.3 s, 168 s.
- The audio at the joins: the largest jump between neighbouring samples
  is 0.17 against a 1.0 peak, speech level; no clicks.
- "Put yourself in it" stills of the three layouts with the pauses on: a
  12 s take with a 1.5 s mute becomes 9 s; the boxed captions pop.
- "Put yourself in it" in full (the same take, two presses forced into
  the plan, a 440 Hz test tone as the song): 12 s became 8.9 s, the
  pointer presses, the boxed captions pop; the tone sits 6 dB over its
  neighbour band, so the music is in the cut sound; no clicks. Render
  224 s headless.
- Live after deploy: /make/ loads in Chrome with no script errors, the
  new module `seg.js` is served, and `/sitemap.xml`, `/robots.txt` and
  `/privacy/` all answer 200.
- Frames of the renders and one clip were put in front of the boss before
  this went live.

## What went wrong
- A name used twice in one function (`at`) stopped the clips script
  loading at all; the first test runs timed out on it. `node --check`
  passed the file because here it doesn't parse `.js` files; checking a
  copy named `.mjs` catches it. All four scripts are checked that way now.
