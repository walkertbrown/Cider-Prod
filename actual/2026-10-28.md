## Sign in with Buffer, and the first check
The boss created the OAuth client (name Tap, confidential; account:read,
posts:read, posts:write, ideas:read, ideas:write). The client id and secret
are in .git-credentials and set as Worker secrets; a bogus-code call to the
token endpoint gave invalid_grant, so the pair is right. Tap's Worker now has
/auth/start (PKCE S256, state kept in KV for 10 minutes) and /auth/callback.
The callback exchanges the code, keeps the tokens in KV under the Buffer
account id, and runs one check: account, channels, sent posts, post metrics,
read templates and tags, and create-then-delete one template and one tag.
Buffer's auth page accepts the request (303 to its login). Waiting for the
boss to sign in once.

## What the sign-in showed
The boss signed in. Buffer granted account:read, posts:read, posts:write,
ideas:read, ideas:write and offline_access, with a refresh token and a

Metrics did not: every post's `metrics` field errors with "Insufficient

for this client. The check's page said "no metrics, 0 of 5"; the real reason
is the scope. Buffer's developer docs and changelog say nothing about
third-party apps getting it; its roadmap has open requests for analytics in
the API. Build step 2 (views and clicks) waits; taps are ours and need none
of it.

## Step 5: long video to clips (live)
- "Or cut a long video into clips" on the studio: pick an MP4 or MOV (up to 60 minutes) and a number of
  clips (1–10; it suggests one per 2 minutes). The video stays in the browser. mp4box.js reads only
  its index (ftyp and moov, wherever moov sits) and WebCodecs decodes only what's needed.
- Sound: decoded in the browser, turned into 16 kHz mono WAV in 10-minute pieces, and sent to
  /api/transcribe, which passes it to whisper-1 for timed words and sentences. Cap: 60 minutes a day per
  account, 600 overall (underCap now counts by amount). /api/moments gives the timed sentences to the
  script model, which picks stand-alone moments of 20–45 s with title, caption and hashtags (one script run).
- Clip edges move to the longest pause within a few seconds, because whisper's words carry no
  punctuation and the first try started mid-sentence ("photo never leaves your phone…").
- Each clip is a card: the words it contains, From/to in seconds, Fit (whole frame on a blurred copy,
  title above) or Fill (crop the middle), captions on or off, title, caption, hashtags. Render, Render all,
  Draft and Draft all work as for the other videos. Over 25 MB can be downloaded but not drafted.
- Tested in Chrome for Testing 156 (Playwright's Chromium can't decode H.264) on a 94 s H.264/AAC talk
  made here with a clock on screen. Two clips found in 9–13 s. A 24 s clip rendered in 88 s and a 41 s
  clip in 149 s (headless, software encode). The clock checks out: clip 0.2 s shows 0:23.5, 12.0 s shows
  0:35.3, 23.5 s shows 0:46.8. Captions follow the speech three words at a time.
- Bug found in the test: every caption was one word, because the gap check mixed whole-video and clip
  times. Fixed.
- Sound in this test came out as Opus, because Chrome on Linux has no AAC encoder. Chrome on Mac and
  Windows makes AAC, as before. Not checked: iPhone HEVC files (Chrome decodes HEVC only where the device
  does; the page says so) and Safari.
- Lost and restored on the way: an edit to make.js dropped the drafting helpers. The local server refused
  to build, and they were put back from git before deploying.
- Privacy page: a section on what is sent (the sound only) and that Tap keeps neither the sound nor the words.
